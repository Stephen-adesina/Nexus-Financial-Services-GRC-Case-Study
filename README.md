# Nexus Financial Services – Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) Review

This project examines the Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) challenges facing a growing financial services organisation with approximately $800 million in assets and no formal GRC framework. It identifies three key organisational weaknesses and recommends practical improvements to strengthen governance, manage business risks and improve regulatory compliance.

## Project Overview

### Project Background

Nexus Financial Services is a growing financial services company that has expanded without establishing a formal Governance, Risk and Compliance framework.

As the organisation grew, business decisions and activities continued without consistent processes for policy approval, risk assessment or regulatory monitoring.

The purpose of this project was to review the organisation's GRC arrangements, identify weaknesses and recommend improvements that would support accountability, effective risk management and regulatory compliance.

### Project Objectives

- Understand the three pillars of Governance, Risk and Compliance.
- Identify organisational governance and compliance weaknesses.
- Assess potential business and operational risks.
- Categorise identified issues under the appropriate GRC pillars.
- Recommend practical controls and improvements.
- Develop a 90-day implementation plan for introducing a basic GRC framework.

### The Three GRC Pillars

**1. Governance**

Establishes how an organisation is directed and controlled, including policies, responsibilities, accountability and management oversight.

**2. Risk Management**

Identifies potential threats, evaluates their likelihood and impact, and determines appropriate actions to reduce or manage risks.

**3. Compliance**

Ensures the organisation follows applicable laws, regulations, standards and internal policies.

### Proposed GRC Framework Structure

The recommended framework consists of three central registers:

- **Policy Register:** Records company policies, policy owners, approval responsibilities, versions and review dates.
- **Risk Register:** Records identified risks, likelihood, impact, ownership, treatment actions and review dates.
- **Compliance Register:** Records regulatory requirements, responsible owners and compliance deadlines.

A designated GRC Coordinator would oversee the framework, report to senior leadership and coordinate regular GRC reviews.

## Configuration Steps

### 1. Reviewed the Organisation's GRC Environment

Examined the case study to understand the organisation's business environment, existing processes and lack of a formal GRC framework.

### 2. Identified the Three GRC Weaknesses

Identified three major issues affecting the organisation:

- No clear process for approving or enforcing company policies.
- No standard risk assessment before business decisions.
- No assigned responsibility for tracking regulatory requirements and deadlines.

### 3. Categorised the Identified Issues

Mapped each issue to its relevant GRC pillar.

| Identified issue | GRC category |
|---|---|
| Lack of policy approval and enforcement | Governance |
| Lack of standard risk assessment | Risk Management |
| Lack of regulatory ownership and deadline monitoring | Compliance |

### 4. Evaluated Potential Business Impacts

Considered how each weakness could affect the organisation.

- **Governance:** Inconsistent working practices and unclear accountability.
- **Risk Management:** Unidentified risks potentially leading to financial losses or reputational damage.
- **Compliance:** Missed regulatory requirements, deadlines, penalties and loss of customer trust.

The case study also referenced the 2023 Meta Ireland enforcement case as an example of how governance, risk management and data privacy compliance weaknesses can combine into a significant failure.

### 5. Developed GRC Recommendations

Recommended introducing a basic organisation-wide GRC framework led by an appointed GRC Coordinator reporting to senior leadership and the Board.

The proposed framework includes:

- A policy register with named owners and approval responsibilities.
- A risk register documenting risks, likelihood, impact, ownership and treatment actions.
- A compliance register tracking regulatory requirements, responsible owners and deadlines.
- Monthly GRC reviews with leadership to identify and escalate outstanding issues.

### 6. Developed a 90-Day Implementation Plan

Proposed a phased approach to introducing the GRC framework.

**First 30 Days**
- Appoint a GRC Coordinator and accountable owners.
- Identify existing policies.
- List key business risks.
- Identify known regulatory requirements.

**Days 31–60**
- Create the three GRC registers.
- Agree on a simple risk scoring method.
- Establish review dates.

**Days 61–90**
- Review the highest-priority risks with leadership.
- Identify overdue compliance requirements.
- Assign corrective actions.
- Monitor and track progress.

## Results and Findings

The review identified that the organisation's main challenge was the absence of a consistent structure for decision-making, risk management and demonstrating compliance.

### Key Findings

**Governance**
- No formal policy approval process.
- Unclear ownership and accountability.
- Lack of consistent policy enforcement.

**Risk Management**
- No standard risk assessment process.
- Business decisions made without consistently evaluating potential risks.
- Lack of structured risk monitoring.

**Compliance**
- No clearly assigned responsibility for regulatory requirements.
- Lack of structured deadline monitoring.
- Potential exposure to penalties and reputational damage.

### Recommended Outcome

The project recommended establishing a simple GRC framework supported by:

- A designated GRC Coordinator.
- Three central registers covering policies, risks and compliance obligations.
- Clear ownership and accountability.
- Regular management reviews.
- Documented corrective actions and escalation procedures.
